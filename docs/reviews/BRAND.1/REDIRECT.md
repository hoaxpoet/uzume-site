# BRAND.1 Task-5 redirect

## Status

Matt rejected the first visual round at Task 5. His direction, verbatim:

> “The mark for C is the strongest, but I dislike the logotype. I don't really like any of the concepts except for C's mark. The color palette is also pretty basic - probably boxes us in for the website and the app. I'm questioning the initial direction.”

When asked whether Direction C's mark should be fixed or used only as a lead, Matt answered:

> “lead”

This note temporarily supersedes DESIGN.md's palette, typography, and single-accent rules while the redirect is unresolved. Product truth, accessibility, abstract-myth sensitivity, voice, and the principle that the engine's output leads remain in force.

## Revised exploration brief

- Treat the five-beat mark as evidence that **arranged energy** resonates; preserve no exact geometry.
- Replace every first-round logotype. New directions use complete, OFL-licensed typefaces with professional spacing and only restrained customization.
- Replace the single-accent law with a **controlled spectrum**: quiet neutral chrome plus a small family of named chromatic roles that can support product UI, website storytelling, contribution metadata, and engine-derived art.
- Shift the visual generator from “first light” to **planned performance**: measure, ensemble, signal, sequence, and orchestration.
- Keep the myth as narrative depth, not a logo-construction recipe.
- Demonstrate every direction in both a website scene and an app-chrome scene before asking for another decision.

## Anti-goals carried forward

No vaporwave or synthwave kit, no DAW skeuomorphism, no crypto glow, no generic AI shimmer, no literal sacred imagery, no decorative motion, and no palette that competes indiscriminately with engine footage.
